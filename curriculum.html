Curriculum & Standards

The Ventura Surf Way — the framework every coach coaches from and every player is measured against, visible to our families all season long.

The Ventura Surf Way — Our Four Pillars

Everything at Ventura Surf is built on the same four pillars — every team, every age, every week. It is the curriculum our coaches coach from and the standard our players are measured against, and it is unique to our club.

Technical

What a player can do with the ball — ball control, passing and first touch, shooting, heading, and 1v1 skills.

Tactical

How a player reads and plays the game — positional play, decision-making, attacking and defensive movement, and set pieces.

Physical

The engine behind the game — endurance, speed, strength, agility, and flexibility, built for the demands of the match.

Mental

The mindset — focus, composure, leadership, adaptability, and competitive spirit, on and off the field.

Our Curriculum — The Season at a Glance

One curriculum for the whole club, January through December. Every month has a purpose, and every week inside it trains all four pillars. This is the plan your player's training follows:

MonthThe Focus
JanuaryFitness base back up — transitions, counter-attacks, and finishing in the final third.
FebruaryPossession and combination play, especially in the attacking third.
MarchTryouts are done — getting every player's individual skill to the same level.
AprilPlayers know what they can do individually; now they learn to play as a unit.
MayAn intense month physically, and a social one — building bonds before the break.
JuneBreak — team camps, optional training, and beach days.
JulyBack at full speed for tournament season, with third training sessions where needed.
AugustThe same push as July, with a close eye on fatigue and injuries after eight hard weeks.
SeptemberHow we defend — as a unit and as individuals — plus small-group combination play.
OctoberCombination play and forward runs — making defenders uncomfortable.
NovemberPossession and pattern play, showcased at the Thanksgiving tournaments and League Cup.
DecemberBreak — time away from the game, then a camp or two before January.

Older teams (U14 and up) follow the same four pillars; December through February is high school season for them, with maintenance work running alongside.

Player Standards

These are the standards our coaches coach and evaluate against — what a Ventura Surf player is working toward at each age. Players progress from Novice to Competent to Proficient to Expert. Pick your player's age band below — you'll only see what applies to them.

Novice Just starting   Competent Getting it   Proficient Game-ready   Expert Mastery

Ages 5–7

1. Technical Skills

Ball Control

  • NoviceDribbling — Maneuvering the ball with close ball control using various parts of the foot.
  • NoviceShielding — Using body positioning to protect the ball from defenders.
  • NoviceClose Control — Executing precise touches to keep the ball close in tight spaces.

Passing/First Touch

  • NoviceShort Passes — Quick and accurate passes to teammates in close proximity.
  • NoviceThrough Balls — Strategically placing passes to break through defensive lines.
  • NoviceFirst Touch — Mastering the art of controlling the ball upon receiving it, setting the stage for the next move.
  • NoviceAerial First Touch — Controlled ball handling in mid-air situations.

Shooting

  • NoviceFinishing — Scoring goals with accuracy using both feet.
  • NoviceVolleys — Striking the ball cleanly while it's in mid-air.
  • NoviceLong-range Shooting — Developing the ability to score from outside the box.

1v1 Skills

  • NoviceSkill Moves — Utilizing effective one-on-one moves to beat defenders.
  • NoviceChange of Direction — Swift changes in direction to outmaneuver opponents.
  • NoviceAcceleration — Quick bursts of speed to leave defenders behind.

2. Tactical Awareness

Positional Play

  • NoviceUnderstanding Formations — Grasping the tactical implications of different team formations.
  • NoviceOccupying Space — Maintaining optimal positions relative to teammates and opponents.
  • NoviceTransitioning — Smoothly shifting between offensive and defensive roles.

Decision-Making

  • NoviceQuick Thinking — Rapidly assessing options and choosing the best course of action.
  • NoviceRisk Assessment — Knowing when to take calculated risks and when to play it safe.
  • NoviceAdaptability — Adjusting strategies based on the evolving dynamics of the game.

Offensive Movement

  • NoviceCreating Space — Making runs to open up space for teammates.
  • NoviceSupporting Attacks — Positioning oneself to provide passing options.
  • NoviceCombination Play — Linking up with teammates through effective passing sequences.

Defensive Skills

  • NoviceMarking — Staying close to opponents to prevent them from receiving or passing the ball.
  • NoviceTackling — Executing clean and effective tackles to regain possession.
  • NoviceInterceptions — Anticipating passes and intercepting them before they reach their target.

Reading the Game

  • NoviceAnticipation — Predicting opponents' movements and decision-making.

Awareness

  • NoviceSpatial Awareness — Understanding positioning in relation to the playing field.

Analytical Skills

  • NoviceQuick Analysis — Rapidly assessing situations and making informed decisions.

3. Physical Fitness

Endurance

  • CompetentCardiovascular Fitness — Building stamina for sustained performance throughout the match.
  • CompetentRecovery — Quick recovery between sprints and intense efforts.

Speed

  • CompetentAcceleration — Rapidly reaching top speed from a stationary position.
  • CompetentSprinting — Maintaining high-speed runs over short distances.

Strength

  • NoviceCore Strength — Stability and power generation through the core.
  • CompetentPhysical Duels — Winning battles for possession through strength and balance.

Agility

  • CompetentQuick Changes of Direction — Nimbly navigating through tight spaces.
  • CompetentAgile Movements — Sharp and responsive movements to outmaneuver opponents.

4. Mental Attributes

Focus

  • NoviceConcentration — Staying focused on the game, opponents, and strategy for the entire match.
  • NoviceBlocking Distractions — Managing external factors to maintain mental clarity.

Composure

  • NoviceCalm Under Pressure — Making sound decisions in high-pressure situations.
  • NoviceHandling Mistakes — Bouncing back from errors with composure and resilience.

Leadership

  • NoviceGuiding Teammates — Vocal communication and directing teammates on the field.
  • NoviceLeading by Example — Demonstrating a strong work ethic and commitment to excellence.

Adaptability

  • NoviceAdjusting to Opponents — Quickly adapting strategies based on the strengths and weaknesses of the opposition.
  • NovicePositional Flexibility — Being adaptable to different roles and positions within the team.

Competitive Spirit

  • CompetentPositive Mindset — Maintaining a competitive edge with a positive and determined mindset.
  • CompetentWinning Mentality — Striving for success and victory in every match.

5. Teamwork and Communication

Communication Skills

  • NoviceClear Communication — Articulating instructions and information concisely.
  • NoviceOn-Field Communication — Constant communication with teammates to coordinate movements.

Collaboration

  • NoviceUnderstanding Roles — Grasping individual responsibilities within team structures.

Supporting Teammates

  • NoviceTeam Cohesion — Fostering a sense of unity and support among teammates.
  • NoviceEncouragement — Providing positive reinforcement and support during challenges.

Understanding Roles

  • NoviceTeam Formation — Grasping the overall strategy and individual roles within the team.
  • NoviceExecuting Team Tactics — Implementing team-specific strategies during matches.
